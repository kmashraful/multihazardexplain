Mastering Collaboration Under Pressure

Mastering Collaboration Under Pressure

The time and budget limits immediately forced high-stakes teamwork, mirroring real-world crisis decision-making.

  • Necessity of Negotiation: Different budgets demanded rapid negotiation to leverage our combined resources effectively.
  • Systems Thinking: We had to think holistically, combining solutions rather than focusing on a single, expensive project.
  • Rapid Prioritization: The 5-minute clock trained us to quickly assess risks and build consensus on the most vital investments.
2

Prioritizing Multi-Hazard Resilience

Prioritizing Multi-Hazard Resilience

The challenge of covering five distinct hazards taught us the critical importance of balanced, integrated strategies.

  • Integrated Solutions: We shifted away from single-hazard fixes, prioritizing measures like Emergency Shelters that offer broad-spectrum protection.
  • Strategic Cost-Benefit: We learned to weigh cost against multi-hazard effectiveness, justifying investments based on comprehensive risk coverage, not just raw power.
  • Value of Soft Measures: Low-cost, "soft" options like Land Use Planning proved essential for building a foundational layer of protection against all threats.
3

Gaining New Knowledge & Strategy

Gaining New Knowledge & Strategy

Analyzing the cost/effectiveness data and experiencing the hazard generator provided practical, risk-informed knowledge.

  • Risk-Informed Spending: We learned to directly tie our budget to the highest probability hazards (like Hurricane and Flood) for maximum impact.
  • Trial-and-Error Feedback: The game provided immediate consequences, allowing us to identify and correct weaknesses in our investment strategy instantly.
  • Developing a Portfolio: We understood that resilience comes from a portfolio of varied investments—both hard infrastructure and community-focused programs.
4

Shifting Our Perspective and Intent

Shifting Our Perspective and Intent

The game's experiential nature fostered social learning and made abstract concepts about resource disparity tangible.

  • Understanding Stakeholder Needs: Differing budgets fostered empathy, showing us how resource constraints shape investment priorities across various community groups.
  • Driving Future Policy Intent: The memorable, high-stress experience is likely to influence our future professional or civic choices toward more balanced, multi-hazard risk reduction.
  • Focus on Systemic Benefit: Our success was measured by the community's overall survival, focusing our attention on broad, systemic improvements over localized projects.
